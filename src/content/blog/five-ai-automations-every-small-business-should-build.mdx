Most small businesses don't need a complex AI strategy to get started. They need a handful of reliable automations that give the team back hours every week.

Here are five that consistently deliver value — and they can all be built with tools like **n8n**, **Make** or **Zapier** plus an LLM.

## 1. Inbox triage and reply drafting

**What it does:** reads incoming emails, labels them (sales, support, invoice, spam), and drafts a suggested reply for common questions.

**Why it matters:** email is often the single biggest time sink for small teams. Even when a person still reviews every reply, starting from a good draft saves significant time.

**Keep a human in the loop:** drafts, not auto-sends — at least until you trust the system.

## 2. Invoice and document data extraction

**What it does:** takes PDFs or images of invoices, receipts or forms, extracts key fields with AI and logs them in a spreadsheet or accounting system.

**Why it matters:** manual data entry is slow and error-prone. AI extraction with validation rules is fast and surprisingly accurate.

## 3. Lead capture and follow-up

**What it does:** collects leads from your website, social media and chat; enriches them; adds them to your CRM; and triggers a personalised follow-up email.

**Why it matters:** speed of response wins deals. Automation makes sure no lead goes cold.

## 4. A FAQ chatbot trained on your content

**What it does:** answers common customer questions on your website using your own FAQs, policies and product information — and hands over to a human when it isn't sure.

**Why it matters:** customers get instant answers outside office hours, and your team handles fewer repetitive questions.

## 5. Weekly business summaries

**What it does:** pulls data from your sales, support and finance tools and writes a short, plain-English summary of the week.

**Why it matters:** decision-makers stay informed without building dashboards or reading reports.

## Getting started

Pick **one** process, map it step by step, and automate the most repetitive part first. Measure the time saved, then move on to the next.
